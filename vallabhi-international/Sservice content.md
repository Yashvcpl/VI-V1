## Credit Assesssment

**What Is Credit Assessment**

Credit assessment is the process of evaluating the creditworthiness of an individual or organization to determine their ability to repay debt. It involves analyzing financial health, repayment capacity, past payment behavior, and key risk factors before extending credit or approving a loan.

The objective is to identify, measure, and manage credit risk so that lenders and investors can make informed decisions on whether to grant credit, and on what terms.

**1\. Retail Credit Assessment**

Evaluate an individual's income, credit history, and repayment capacity to determine loan eligibility.

**2\. MSME & Business Credit Assessment**

Analyze business financials, cash flow, and repayment ability to support informed lending decisions.

**3\. Corporate Credit Assessment**

Assess financial strength, business risk, and creditworthiness for medium and large enterprises.

**How Credit Assessment Works**

The process typically begins with collecting and verifying financial and business information, including financial statements, tax filings, bank records, and credit bureau data. This is followed by a detailed analysis of revenue, profitability, liquidity, solvency, cash flows, and existing debt to assess financial stability and repayment capacity.

Based on this analysis, the borrower is assigned a risk grade or score, and appropriate credit terms are determined, such as credit limit, tenor, interest rate, covenants, and security requirements. Credit assessment is not a one-time exercise; ongoing monitoring of payment behavior and financial performance helps manage risk over the life of the exposure.

## What Is Private Equity

Private equity (PE) is an investment approach where firms raise capital from institutional and accredited investors to buy ownership stakes in companies that are not publicly listed, or to take public companies private. The goal is to actively manage and improve these businesses over several years and then sell them at a higher value.

## How Private Equity Works

Private equity firms (general partners) collect funds from investors such as pension funds, endowments, insurance companies, and wealthy individuals (limited partners). They use this capital, often combined with debt, to acquire controlling or significant stakes in target companies.guides.library.harvard+4

After acquisition, PE firms work closely with management to improve operations, cut costs, reposition strategy, strengthen governance, and sometimes pursue add‑on acquisitions. Once value has been created-usually over 3 to 10 years-the firm exits through a sale to another company, another investor, or via an IPO.
